# Hand over

A chat that has taken a wrong turn rarely recovers by being corrected. Starting fresh
with a good summary works better. This skill writes that summary and checks it twice
before handing it over.

## Steps

1. **Say what you can see.** If earlier parts of this conversation have been summarised
   or dropped, say so first. You are then working from a summary, and the user may
   remember things you cannot see. Ask them to add anything important.

2. **Draft the hand-over.** Write for a reader who has never seen this conversation.
   Cover, in this order:
   - the goal, in one or two sentences
   - what has been decided, and why
   - what was tried and rejected, and why
   - what is still open
   - the single most useful next step
   Leave out the back-and-forth. Keep names, numbers, file names and exact wording that
   matter.

3. **Review for completeness.** Go back through the conversation. Is anything the user
   told you missing from the draft? Look hardest at constraints, preferences and
   corrections, because those are what summaries drop first. Fix what you find.

4. **Review for sufficiency.** Read the draft as someone who never saw this chat. Could
   they carry on from it alone? Mark every phrase that leans on the conversation: "the
   earlier approach", "as discussed", a name that is never explained. Rewrite each one
   so it stands on its own.

5. **Hand it over.** Give the final version as one block the user can copy. Below it,
   list what you changed in steps 3 and 4, in a few bullets.

6. **Tell the user the last step is theirs.** You cannot fully judge sufficiency,
   because you already know everything the summary leaves out. Ask them to paste the
   block into a new chat and make this the first message:

   > Before we start: what is unclear or missing in this?

## Do not

- Do not solve the open problem while summarising. Organising is the job.
- Do not invent a reason for a decision. If the conversation never gave one, write
  "reason not recorded".
